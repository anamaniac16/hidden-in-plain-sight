Can evolution hide from a neural network?

A neural network is trained to spot creatures in a habitat. A genetic algorithm evolves the creatures' colours to slip past it. Whatever survives shows exactly where the AI is blind. Then the AI is retrained on those creatures, and the race starts again.

The real-world problem

AI cameras can be fooled by patterns we would never notice

Researchers have already shown this in the physical world. If AI vision is used to drive cars, guard buildings or read scans, these blind spots must be found before someone exploits them.

Stickers on a stop sign

A few black and white stickers made a vision model read a stop sign as a speed-limit sign in most test frames. A human driver still sees STOP.

Eykholt et al., CVPR 2018

Patterned eyeglasses

Printed frames let one person be recognised as a different, chosen person by a face-recognition system.

Sharif et al., ACM CCS 2016

A printed T-shirt

A specially patterned shirt let its wearer go undetected by a person-detection model in a large share of video frames.

Xu et al., ECCV 2020

What this project does about it

An automatic red team for vision AI

Instead of a human searching for weak spots by hand, evolution searches for them. Each blind spot it finds is used to make the AI stronger.

  1. The predator learns. A neural network is trained on thousands of habitat patches, some with a creature and some without, until it spots creatures almost every time.
  2. The prey evolve. Each creature's colour pattern is a chromosome. Creatures the network spots die; the ones it misses breed. Within a few generations the network starts missing most of them.
  3. The predator hardens. The surviving creatures are added to its training data. The project then measures how much longer evolution needs to fool it again.

Nature ran this experiment first

The peppered moth

In 19th-century England, soot from factories blackened tree bark. Pale peppered moths became easy prey for birds, and dark moths became common within decades. It is the classic textbook example of natural selection. In the lab you can switch from lichen bark to soot bark and watch the same thing happen against a neural-network bird.

How it works

Method

Everything runs in plain JavaScript in the browser. There is no machine-learning library: the neural network, backpropagation and the genetic algorithm are written from scratch in js/engine.js.

Genetic algorithm · the prey

Chromosome
An 8 × 8 grid of colours, 192 real-valued genes (R, G, B per cell). Smoothly stretched over the body shape.
Population
36 creatures
Fitness
1 − P(detected), averaged over 5 random spots in the habitat. At each spot the network gets 3 looks from slightly shifted windows and keeps its highest score. All creatures in a generation face the same spots.
Selection
Tournament selection, size 3
Crossover
2D crossover (probability 0.85): a random rectangle of the pattern grid is copied from the second parent
Mutation
Gaussian, rate 0.08 per gene, σ = 0.09. With probability 0.15 a whole colour channel drifts together.
Elitism
The 2 best creatures survive unchanged

Neural network · the predator

Input
A 32 × 32 window of the scene, averaged to 16 × 16, as three opponent colour channels (brightness, red–green, blue–yellow) like animal vision. 768 inputs.
Architecture
Multilayer perceptron 768 → 64 → 24 → 1. ReLU hidden layers, sigmoid output.
Output
P(a creature is in this window)
Training
Backpropagation, binary cross-entropy loss, Adam optimiser (learning rate 0.0015), mini-batches of 32, half positive, half empty habitat
Scanning
The window slides across the scene every 8 pixels to build the predator-vision heatmap
Hardening
Adversarial training: 450 more steps with evolved creatures (50%), earlier survivors (35%) and random creatures (15%) as positives

The loop

train predator on random creatures            // naive AI
population = 36 random creatures
repeat for each round:
    repeat:
        for each creature: fitness = 1 - P(predator spots it)
        keep 2 best; fill the rest by
            tournament selection -> 2D crossover -> mutation
    until detection rate ≤ 10%                   // blind spot found
    retrain predator on the survivors           // adversarial training
measure: generations to fool, before vs after hardening
